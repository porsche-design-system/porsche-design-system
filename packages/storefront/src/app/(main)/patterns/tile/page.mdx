Tiles and content cards group the content of a single item – a model, a category, a product or a feature – in an area of
its own. They vary too much in layout, media and interaction to be covered by a single component, so these patterns show
their common use cases, built with HTML, Tailwind CSS and the components of the Porsche Design System. Start from the
pattern closest to your use case and adapt it – the comments in its code explain why it is built the way it is.

The two groups below differ in how a reader reaches what they offer. Hovering anywhere on a tile reaches at least one
link, so the whole tile is clickable. A content card presents its content in a container, and what a reader can do with
it – open a page, press a button, read on – sits in its elements rather than in the surface as a whole. Within each
group, the patterns are ordered by what a reader can do with them rather than by what they show. Where the photograph
sits is visible in every example below; whether the whole area is one link is not, and it is the decision that shapes
the markup, the focus order and what a screen reader announces.

## Tiles

Every tile below opens one page, and the whole of it is the clickable area. Two forms get it there.

<H3Anchored>Image Category</H3Anchored>

The first group shows a link, and a `p-link` with `cover` opens the page: the link is part of the tile and its clickable
area is stretched over the whole of it, which also draws the focus indicator around the tile. Use this form wherever the
tile shows a link anyway.

## Content Cards

A content card shows its content in a container, and what a reader can do with it sits in its elements rather than in
the surface as a whole – no link spans a content card the way it spans a tile above.

<H3Anchored>Vehicle Listing</H3Anchored>

This card carries several controls of its own – a popover, a link to the technical data and two actions – so no link
could span it, and it does not react to hover as a whole either. The heading names the vehicle, the actions open it.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/vehicle-listing" />

<H3Anchored>Equipment</H3Anchored>

The next three cards open nothing, and what they offer instead differs. This one carries the single action of its
subject, a button, because an equipment option is something to add rather than a page to visit. Its photograph takes two
thirds of the card and the description the rest, which is why the two stack on a phone.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/equipment" />

<H3Anchored>Disclosure</H3Anchored>

This one reveals more of itself on demand: its heading is the summary of an accordion that holds the details, and the
photograph behind the text dims while they are open. Nothing leaves the card, so nothing has to be a link.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/disclosure" />

<H3Anchored>Equipment Stacked</H3Anchored>

This one only describes. It is the equipment card with its two panels stacked rather than beside one another, for a
column too narrow for the two halves, and it carries no action.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/equipment-stacked" />

<H3Anchored>Support</H3Anchored>

This one holds three tiles instead of opening one itself: a mood image fills it, and in front of the image three small
tiles each open one way to reach a person. They take a frosted background, which lets the photograph through. The card
holding them opens nothing itself – a link may not contain another.
