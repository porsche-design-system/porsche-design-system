A tile groups the content of a single item – a model, a category, a product or a feature – in an area that is often
clickable as a whole. Tiles vary too much in layout, media and interaction to be covered by a single component, so these
patterns show their common use cases, built with HTML, Tailwind CSS and the components of the Porsche Design System.
Start from the pattern closest to your use case and adapt it – the comments in its code explain why it is built the way
it is.

They are ordered by what a reader can do with a tile rather than by what it shows. Where the photograph sits is visible
in every example below; whether the whole tile is one link is not, and it is the decision that shapes the markup, the
focus order and what a screen reader announces.

## Image Category

The tiles in this first group each open one page, and a `p-link` with `cover` opens it: the link is part of the tile and
its clickable area is stretched over the whole of it, which also draws the focus indicator around the tile. Use this
form wherever the tile shows a link anyway.

## Model Navigation

The next three tiles open a page as well, but show no link. An anchor of their own spans the tile, and its text is for
screen readers only – the heading names the subject in the design, the anchor names it for assistive technology. Use
this form where a visible link would be one element too many.

## Equipment

The last three tiles open nothing, and what they offer instead differs. This one carries the single action of its
subject, a button, because an equipment option is something to add rather than a page to visit. Its photograph takes two
thirds of the tile and the description the rest, which is why the two stack on a phone.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/equipment" />

## Disclosure

This one reveals more of itself on demand: its heading is the summary of an accordion that holds the details, and the
photograph behind the text dims while they are open. Nothing leaves the tile, so nothing has to be a link.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/disclosure" />

## Equipment Stacked

This one only describes. It is the equipment tile with its two panels stacked rather than beside one another, for a
column too narrow for the two halves, and it carries no action.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/equipment-stacked" />

## Category Grid

The patterns above each show a single tile. This one shows three of them under a shared heading, with the first tile as
tall as the other two together — a focus without making any of the three a different kind of tile. The tile itself is
the category tile, unchanged, so what this pattern really shows is an arrangement.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/category-grid" />

## Support

This one holds three tiles rather than standing beside them: a mood image fills it, and in front of the image three
small tiles each open one way to reach a person. They take a frosted background, which lets the photograph through. The
tile holding them opens nothing itself – a link may not contain another.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/support" />
