A tile groups the content of a single item – a model, a category, a product or a feature – in a rounded area that is
often clickable as a whole. These patterns show the use cases of the [Tile](/components/tile/examples) component built
with plain HTML and Tailwind CSS instead, for layouts the component doesn't cover or setups without it. The content
still uses the components of the Porsche Design System.

The patterns share a few techniques. A clickable tile is the positioned ancestor (`relative`) of its link: either a
`p-link` with `cover` stretches its clickable area over the tile and draws the focus ring around it, with the corners
set via `[--p-link-cover-radius:var(--radius-3xl)]`, or an anchor of its own spans the tile via `absolute inset-0`, with
its label as screen reader text. Independent actions are placed above the link via `relative z-2`. Hover effects are
`group-hover:` variants of the tile, wrapped in `motion-safe:` to respect the reduced motion preference of the user.
Media zooming on hover is clipped by a wrapper with `overflow-clip` rather than by a clickable tile itself, which would
clip the focus ring of its link as well. Gradients such as `bg-fade-to-t` ensure the legibility of text on images.

The page follows the color scheme of the user via `scheme-light-dark`, and so do most tiles: their photos fill their
area and their cut-outs have a transparent background, so they work on a light and a dark surface alike. Only where the
content asks for a fixed scheme, the tile sets it: `scheme-only-dark` for light content on a gradient, a background
image or a brand color, and `scheme-only-light` for the category, whose dark vehicle would vanish on a dark surface.
