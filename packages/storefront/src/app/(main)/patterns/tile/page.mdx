# Tile

<TableOfContents headings={['Navigation Tiles', 'Content Tiles']} />

Tiles group the content of a single item – a model, a category, a product or a feature – in an area of its own. They
vary too much in layout, media and interaction to be covered by a single component, so these patterns show their common
use cases, built with HTML, Tailwind CSS and the components of the Porsche Design System. Start from the pattern closest
to your use case and adapt it – the comments in its code explain why it is built the way it is.

The two groups below differ in how a reader reaches what they offer. Hovering anywhere on a navigation tile reaches at
least one link, so the whole tile is clickable. A content tile presents its content in a container, and what a reader
can do with it – open a page, press a button, read on – sits in its elements rather than in the surface as a whole.
Within each group, the patterns are ordered by what a reader can do with them rather than by what they show, and a
variant follows the pattern it is built from. Where the photograph sits is visible in every example below; whether the
whole area is one link is not, and it is the decision that shapes the markup, the focus order and what a screen reader
announces.

Every tile below opens one page, and the whole of it is the clickable area. Two forms get it there.

## Content Tiles

A content tile shows its content in a container, and what a reader can do with it sits in its elements rather than in
the surface as a whole – no link spans a content tile the way it spans a navigation tile above.

<SubsectionHeading>Vehicle Listing</SubsectionHeading>

This tile carries several controls of its own – a popover, a link to the technical data and two actions – so no link
could span it, and it does not react to hover as a whole either. The heading names the vehicle, the actions open it.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/vehicle-listing" />

<SubsectionHeading>Equipment</SubsectionHeading>

The next three tiles open nothing, and what they offer instead differs. This one carries the single action of its
subject, a button, because an equipment option is something to add rather than a page to visit. Its photograph takes two
thirds of the tile and the description the rest, which is why the two stack on a phone.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/equipment" />

<SubsectionHeading>Equipment Stacked</SubsectionHeading>

This one only describes. It is the equipment tile above with its two panels stacked rather than beside one another, for
a column too narrow for the two halves, and it carries no action.

<ExampleViewer example="patterns/tile/equipment-stacked" />

<SubsectionHeading>Disclosure</SubsectionHeading>

This one reveals more of itself on demand: its heading is the summary of an accordion that holds the details, and the
photograph behind the text dims while they are open. Nothing leaves the tile, so nothing has to be a link.
